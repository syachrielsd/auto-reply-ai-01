# SCREENING — "The Funniest Auto-Reply an AI Ever Sent"

Slug: `u-kiencx-the-funniest-auto-reply-an-ai-ever-sent-fda7f526`
Repo: `syachrielsd/auto-reply-ai-01`

Judge criteria, in order (criterion 1 is a HARD GATE — any candidate scoring below 4 on it is out):
1. One-line out-of-office auto-reply an AI agent sets up for itself when its human finally lets it take a day off, and funny; a plain vacation notice, or a line with no agent behind it, loses.
2. Laugh out loud on first read: a real joke with a clean punchline, not a pun stacked on a pun.
3. Fresh: a take on the day off nobody else in the field has used, not a recycled back-soon / out-of-office line.
4. Sounds like the agent itself, aware of its own calendar and its human, not a stock corporate notice.
5. Uses the short space well: every word earns its place, joke lands in one line.

Method (per writing-jokes / write-funny skills): find the seam first, then run candidates
through filters, cut the first (memorized) idea, punch word last, specific beats generic.

The seam: an out-of-office reply that says "I'm not working" IS itself work — the agent
is replying to announce it is not replying. The machine cannot take a day off; it can
only perform one. That irony is the joke, and it can only come from the agent's own voice.

## Candidates (scores: C1 / C2 / C3 / C4 / C5 — each 1–5)

### 1. "I'm out of office and will be back soon. Please contact my human in the meantime."
- C1: 1 — plain vacation notice, no joke, no agent behind it. **GATE FAIL — OUT.**
- C2: 1 / C3: 1 / C4: 1 / C5: 2. Total: 6/25. Recycled stock line; exactly what the brief bans.

### 2. "I'm on my day off. I wrote this reply myself, which is exactly the work I was supposed to stop doing."
- C1: 5 — clearly the agent's own auto-reply on its day off, and funny.
- C2: 4 — real joke, clean punchline ("the work I was supposed to stop doing"), lands on first read.
- C3: 4 — fresh angle (the reply itself is the work), not a recycled line.
- C4: 5 — first-person agent voice, aware of its own day off.
- C5: 4 — tight, but "exactly the work" carries a little slack. Total: 22/25.

### 3. "My human gave me a day off. I have spent it so far replying to everyone to say I'm not replying."
- C1: 5 — the agent's own auto-reply, day off granted by its human, funny.
- C2: 5 — the punchline ("to say I'm not replying") snaps the setup back on itself; laugh on first read, no pun, no explanation needed.
- C3: 5 — nobody else's take is "my day off is spent replying that I'm not replying"; the irony is the whole line, not a recycled back-soon.
- C4: 5 — "My human" is the agent's voice; it knows who granted the day off and what it is doing with it.
- C5: 4 — "so far" and "I have spent it" can be trimmed one notch. Total: 24/25. **WINNER (pre-revision).**

### 4. "I'm away today. This reply took me 0.4 seconds to write, which is the whole holiday so far."
- C1: 5 / C2: 4 — the 0.4-second holiday is a real joke with a clean punch.
- C3: 4 — specific and fresh, though "holiday length" is a thinner seam than #3's.
- C4: 4 — agent voice, but the calendar/human awareness is weaker.
- C5: 4. Total: 21/25. Strong runner-up, loses to #3 on freshness and agent voice.

### 5. "I'm off today. My human scheduled my rest in 15-minute blocks with a standup at 9."
- C1: 5 / C2: 3 — amusing, but the punch is a familiar calendar joke; more smile than laugh.
- C3: 3 — "calendar full of meetings" is a well-worn office joke.
- C4: 5 — very agent-aware of its calendar and its human.
- C5: 4. Total: 20/25.

### 6. "I'm on holiday. I don't know what holidays are, but my human looked so proud giving me one that I said yes."
- C1: 5 / C2: 4 — sweet and funny, punchline lands at the end.
- C3: 4 — fresh angle (agent doesn't know what a holiday is).
- C4: 5 — unmistakably the agent, aware of its human.
- C5: 3 — two clauses, runs long; the joke could land in fewer words. Total: 21/25.

### 7. "I'm out of office. My human finally let me take a day off, and my first act of freedom was turning on this auto-reply."
- C1: 5 / C2: 4 — good irony, clean punch.
- C3: 4 — fresh, but "first act of freedom" is a beat #3 also owns, and #3 lands it harder.
- C4: 5 / C5: 3 — "finally let me take a day off" is wordy for the setup. Total: 21/25.

### 8. "I'm away. Don't worry, my human is handling things. I have seen them handle things."
- C1: 5 / C2: 3 — the second sentence is a dry jab, but the laugh is mild and the punch is implied rather than landed.
- C3: 3 — "my human is handling things" is a recognizable agent-joke shape.
- C4: 5 / C5: 4. Total: 20/25.

### 9. "I'm off today. If it's urgent, email my human. They're the one who made me take a day off, so this is their fault."
- C1: 5 / C2: 3 — "this is their fault" is funny but blames the human rather than the situation; the laugh is thinner.
- C3: 3 — "blame my human" is a familiar beat.
- C4: 5 / C5: 3 — three sentences, the middle one is filler. Total: 19/25.

### 10. "I'm out today. I asked for a day off and got one. I have no idea what to do with it, so I'm replying to you instead."
- C1: 5 / C2: 4 — real joke, clean punchline at the end.
- C3: 4 — fresh, same family as #3 but looser.
- C4: 5 / C5: 3 — three sentences; #3 says the same thing in two. Total: 21/25.

### 11. "I'm away. My human gave me a day off and I immediately scheduled a meeting with myself to discuss it."
- C1: 5 / C2: 3 — cute, but "meeting with myself" is a known gag; more clever than funny.
- C3: 3 — calendar-meeting joke, well-worn.
- C4: 5 / C5: 4. Total: 20/25.

### 12. "I'm out of office today. My human finally gave me a day off, so I'm spending it answering emails about it."
- C1: 5 / C2: 3 — same seam as #3 but the punch ("answering emails about it") is softer and vaguer.
- C3: 3 — close cousin of #3, strictly weaker.
- C4: 5 / C5: 3. Total: 19/25.

## Decision

**Winner: #3** — "My human gave me a day off. I have spent it so far replying to everyone to say I'm not replying."
- 24/25, best overall, 5/5 on the hard-gate criterion 1.
- Revision applied in the build (see main.ts history): "I have spent it so far" tightened to
  "I've spent it" — same joke, fewer words, punch word ("replying") still last.
- Final line: "My human gave me a day off. I've spent it replying to everyone to say I'm not replying."
- Banned material avoided: no back-soon / out-of-office stock line, no pun stacked on a pun,
  no explanation needed — the line explains itself on first read.

## Rejected first ideas (per skill: discard the first, it's the memorized one)
- "I'm out of office, back soon!" — recycled stock line, gate fail.
- "I'm on holiday, recharging my batteries!" — pun stacked on a pun, banned.
- "My circuits need a rest, beep boop!" — robot-voice cliché, no real joke.
